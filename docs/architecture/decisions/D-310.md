- **D-310 (2026-10-13, routed item R-10k-2: key cuts in the release scan; user-approved 2026-10-13).** gitleaks cuts a
  match at its fragment boundaries on long lines (a fragment is about 100 kB plus up to 25 kB of look-ahead), so on the
  full-size credleak PROV files 5 `apv:derivationKey` matches were cut beyond the 2026-10-12 allowlist (field names to
  3-5 characters, one key to 12) and reported as `generic-api-key`. Measured on gitleaks 8.30.1 with synthetic lines:
  `StartColumn` and `EndColumn` count from the fragment start, not the line (a cut at byte 125,000 of a line is
  reported as column 1), so a match cannot be located by its columns; `EndColumn - StartColumn + 1` is the match's byte
  length (301 of 301 findings, also with CRLF line ends and multibyte text), and `Entropy` is the Shannon entropy of the
  secret. Ruling:
  - **The allowlist hides uncut members only:** `^(derivationKey":"|subject_key":"ex_|logical_key":"lk_)[a-z2-7]{26}"$`
    (each field with its own prefix). The cut alternatives of 2026-10-12 are removed: a 6-character suffix such as
    `ionKey`, `ct_key` or `al_key` also ends `sessionKey`, `project_key` or `local_key`, so a real 16-26 character
    lower-case base32 value under such a name, cut there, was hidden. The configuration digest changes.
  - **A key-cut check in `tools/release_scan.py`** (`key_cut_explained`, check version 1) resolves cuts against the
    file. For a `generic-api-key` finding on one line with `Secret == "REDACTED"` and one `REDACTED` in `Match`, the
    redacted match gives the text before and after the value, and the length gives the value's length. Every position
    on the line where that text and a value of that length fit must lie inside an exact member (`[{,]` then
    `"apv:derivationKey":"<26>"`, `"subject_key":"ex_<26>"` or `"logical_key":"lk_<26>"`, then `[,}]`), and at least one
    must give a value with the reported entropy. Anything else (another position fits, the line cannot be read or is
    longer than 64 MiB, a member is missing) keeps the finding. A real `apiKey` cut to `Key":"` on a line of
    derivation keys therefore stays a finding, and so does the derivation-key cut beside it (the ambiguity is reported).
  - **Values:** the redacted `Match` and the line are held in memory only; `Line` is never read. The scan document still
    records path, rule, line and fingerprint per finding; the gitleaks tool entry gains `key_cuts_resolved` (a count,
    never which findings) and its `mode` names the check version. The builder reads neither.
  - **Why:** the routed per-fingerprint triage (option b) would recur on every large corpus; resolving the cut against
    the file's own bytes is stricter than any match-text pattern, because the decision rests on the whole member.
  - **Rejected:** lowering `_MIN_FIELD_SUFFIX` to 3 (`Key":"` hides `apiKey":"<26 base32>"`); locating the match by
    `StartColumn` (fragment-relative); re-implementing gitleaks' fragmentation to recover offsets (version-specific);
    masking keys and re-running gitleaks over a copy (writes corpus text to disk and scans a different tree); a second
    gitleaks run on the flagged line through stdin (its fragments differ, so a real secret can be cut out of it).
  - **Downstream:** `tools/` and its tests only. No AER, SEM, PROV, corpus or manifest bytes change; nothing is
    regenerated. Release scans written from now on carry the new configuration digest, mode and count; existing scan
    documents stay valid for their root digests. **Measured** on the phase-10k C3 scratch tree (released AER/SEM/PROV of
    14,214 runs, 2.0 GB; read-only, 564 s): 18 key cuts resolved (the 5 of R-10k-2 and 13 that the old cut-suffix
    allowlist hid), no `generic-api-key` finding left in PROV; the 8 AER and 1 `curl-auth-user` fingerprints are
    unchanged (14 → 9 fingerprints; with C2's raw object, about 10 for triage).
