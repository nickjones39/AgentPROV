- **D-12 (2026-10-08, artefact catalogues for every source; user requirement, architect ruling).** Every source that
  becomes an AgentPROV PROV corpus gets a versioned artefact catalogue (`adapters/<family>/artefacts.toml`). The
  sources include the AgentDojo legacy runs (the converted corpus), the AgentDojo-PROV trees, ROPE and AutoDojo, the
  NIST mirror, AgentDyn, Mandate, DRIFT, the survey sources, CIAware-Bench, STAC and live capture.
  - **Mechanism:** the handling is **per source and bespoke**. Each adapter owns its catalogue and handles every
    edge case at conversion time, through its own mapper, profile and SEM rules; the pattern repeats in every
    input source. A thin shared layer, `agentprov.artefacts` (L3; it changes no protected package), holds only the
    catalogue schema, a source-neutral safety net over AER and the reports. Hits that no catalogue entry claims are recorded as `artefact.unclassified.*`, never
    dropped. Inspection is proactive (`import --dry-run --artefacts`) and reporting is per-source (`stats
    --artefacts`, numbers-only reports feeding the manuscript).
  - **Cleaning** is limited to five declared classes (D.15.1): correct representation, derived reconstruction by a
    declared rule, recomputed judgements beside the recorded ones (SEM and the assessment bundle), flag only, and
    not recoverable. Raw bytes, AER source values and the compat oracle are never changed.
  - **Converted AgentDojo runs:** their handling is recorded entry by entry in D.15.5.
  - **Rejected:**
    - "cleaning" by rewriting values or dropping traces: it destroys evidence and creates selection artefacts;
    - per-adapter ad-hoc flags with no shared catalogue schema: the counts would not be comparable across sources,
      nor citable in the paper;
    - one central artefact engine holding source knowledge: it would break invariants 1 and 9.
