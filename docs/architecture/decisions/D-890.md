- **D-890 (2026-10-28, `withhold_scan` reads stored archives in parallel; M5 Ultra performance round 2, item 5).**
  - **Finding.** `corpus/withhold_scan.py` read one root archive at a time, and each container's `expand` was one
    `WorkerPool.run`: a worker started, one job ran and the worker retired. So the worker count never mattered: 72
    synthetic archives took 44–48 s at 4 and at 36 workers (main 49898828).
  - **Change.** Up to `workers` roots are read at once.
    - Each root is read by a reader with its own scratch store and its own one-worker pool, reused root after root.
    - One root's depth-first walk and its decode budget (D-452) stay sequential.
    - Split descriptors are still read first, because a part is skipped only once its descriptor was read cleanly.
    - Outcomes are recorded in the serial order, so `DecodedScan` is identical at every worker count, iteration order
      included.
    - `member_scan` calls are serialised (D-491's callback in `tools/release_scan.py` sets `emails.where` before each
      search). Their order across roots is no longer fixed, and both callers only gather sets.
    - `in_process` keeps one reader.
    - A reused walk's `partial` flag is reset per root. Before, it could leak into a root whose bytes failed to
      store.
  - **Measured** (same worker code, 3 repeats, load average 8–19): workers 1 44.9 s, 4 13.9 s, 16 7.3 s, 36 10.0 s.
    Every point has the same `DecodedScan` fingerprint.
  - **Rejected:**
    - Keeping workers alive across `WorkerPool.run` calls: it helps every caller, but `io/workers.py` is in the AER
      stage scope (class 1).
    - Containers of one root in parallel: the walk and its budget are sequential by definition.
    - The rule search in the worker: it is not the cost, and it would change what crosses the T1 boundary.
  - **Class 0:** `corpus/withhold_scan.py` is outside every stage scope.
