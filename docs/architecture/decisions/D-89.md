- **D-89 (2026-10-12, tier-4 round trip: one encoding and one parse per serialisation, released early; step 1 of the
  phase-11 hot-spot proposals).** `validate/roundtrip.py` now encodes the document and writes its TriG once (under the
  surrogate names). It parses that text once and uses the parsed dataset for three purposes: the model decode, which
  is what `ProvDocument.deserialize` does with the same text; the graph comparison; and the completeness check, which
  reads the image with each surrogate namespace mapped back to its real IRI. Before, completeness re-encoded and
  re-parsed the real-named document, and the first TriG text was parsed twice. The first model is released before
  the second is encoded, and the graphs are compared one named graph at a time. The TriG text round trip itself is
  unchanged.
  - **Evidence:** 30 executions (the largest and median AERs of every corpus, re-mapped from raw; two DTap runs of
    5.8 and 14.7 MB of PROV) give byte-identical tier-4 reports before and after. The validation unit and PROV suites
    pass. Interleaved timing of the round trip on a shared machine: ROPE 2.5 MB PROV 21–26 s → 11–17 s (304 → 278 MB),
    DTap 5.8 MB 37–67 s → 24–36 s (peak ~590 MB, unchanged), head 0.8 MB about the same.
  - **Not solved by step 1:** the DTap peak (1.5–1.7 GB for 14.7 MB of PROV). Proposals 2 (N-Quads instead of TriG)
    and 3 (comparing canonical N-Quads lines instead of a second decode) change which serialisation the check
    exercises, so they were evaluated separately (step 2 below).
  - **Rejected:** comparing the in-memory encoding instead of the parsed text (it would stop testing the TriG writer
    and parser).
  - **Step 2 (addendum, 2026-10-12; proposals 2 and 3 adopted).** The round trip writes N-Quads instead of TriG and
    parses only the first text: the decode and the completeness check read that parse, and the second encoding is
    compared with the first as sets of N-Quads lines per graph, not parsed. On the largest DTap run the TriG writer
    took 8.3 s per text and the N-Quads writer 0.9 s; rdflib parses N-Quads more slowly (7.7 s against 5.5 s), so
    only one text is parsed. The texts are kept as bytes. The parsed image gets the encoding's namespace bindings,
    which a TriG text declared: the decoder names IRIs by them, so a failed model comparison names records with the
    document's prefixes, not minted `ns1:` ones. A blank node counts as lost and added, as it did when both texts were
    parsed. Completeness runs after the first model is released and before the second encoding.
    - **Evidence:** the 30 executions give byte-identical tier-4 reports to those before D-89 (tier 4 381 s → 132 s in total for the 30, under load; step 1 gave 305 s). 81 crafted documents (control
      characters, quotes, `>`, lone surrogates, odd local names and bundle names) give the outcomes of step 1 except
      three, all with a raw line break in a name: TriG writes such an IRI unescaped and its parser accepts it; N-Quads
      rejects it, so step 2 reports `pv.roundtrip.load`. The tier-4 structure check already rejects every such name
      (`pv.prov_structure.qname`), so no document that passes it changes. Process CPU time, step 1 → step 2,
      interleaved under load: ROPE 2.5 MB PROV 6.7–8.2 → 5.0–5.3 s, head 0.8 MB 2.2 → 1.5 s, adprov 13 → 9.3 s,
      DTap 14.7 MB 44–55 → 41–45 s. DTap peak RSS of the round trip 1,509–1,579 → 1,302–1,419 MB.
    - **What it no longer tests:** the TriG writer and parser. TriG stays the on-demand output (`derive --trig`,
      E.5.4) and is not part of the sealed corpus; the round trip now tests the N-Quads writer and parser.
    - **Still not solved:** most of the DTap peak is the first model held together with its rdflib encoding
      (~0.85 GB); the `prov` API encodes from a whole model. The worker RSS cap (`worker.py`) is phase 12's and is
      unchanged.
    - **Rejected:** parsing the second text too (7.7 s on DTap for no change in any finding); hashing the lines
      (smaller, but equality would no longer be exact).
